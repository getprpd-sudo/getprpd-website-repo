# PRPD Meal-Prep Margin Benchmarks

Last updated: July 22, 2026

Purpose: explain whether PRPD's current margins are healthy, distinguish startup cash retained from true profit, and define the financial controls needed for future weekly closeouts.

## Plain-Language Conclusion

PRPD's current **food and direct variable costs are healthy**. Batch 3 uses an estimated 28.6% of booked revenue for food and 32.7% for all currently known cash operating costs, including packaging, consumables, and delivery fuel. That leaves a 67.3% startup cash contribution before owner labor, advertising, fixed overhead, waste, refunds, and taxes.

The 67.3% figure is **not net profit**. It is the cash available to pay the currently unmeasured costs and then retain earnings in the business. PRPD cannot calculate a defensible fully loaded net margin until actual production hours, batch-specific marketing, waste, and recurring overhead are recorded.

## External Benchmarks

These figures provide context, not mandatory targets:

1. The National Restaurant Association reported that food and nonalcoholic beverages represented a median **32.4% of sales** for limited-service restaurants in 2024. PRPD's current food-use ratio is 28.6%.
2. The Association reported that limited-service prime costs, meaning food plus labor, represented a median **65% of sales**, while median income before taxes was **4.0% of sales**. This shows why a healthy food cost does not automatically mean a high net profit.
3. Limited-service restaurant occupancy costs represented a median **5.2% of sales** in 2024. PRPD currently avoids a comparable recurring storefront cost, which is a real startup advantage.
4. HelloFresh, whose portfolio includes Factor ready-to-eat meals, reported a Q1 2025 group contribution margin excluding impairment of **27.0%**. Its ready-to-eat category reported an EBITDA margin of **-8.4%** for that quarter. These numbers are not directly comparable to PRPD, but they demonstrate that production labor, fulfillment, marketing, and overhead can consume a large portion of an apparently strong meal margin.
5. The U.S. Small Business Administration defines contribution margin as selling price minus variable cost and recommends using it to calculate break-even sales. It also recommends allowing for unpredictable expenses rather than assuming every remaining dollar is profit.

## Labor-Adjusted Scenarios

The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported an average DFW wage of $15.69 per hour for food-preparation and serving occupations in May 2024. This is used only as a labor-value benchmark; PRPD is not currently paying owner wages.

| Total weekly owner/production hours | Imputed labor at $15.69/hour | Remaining contribution | Margin before marketing, overhead, tax |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 20 hours | $313.80 | $650.56 | 45.4% |

This sensitivity table shows that the current model can support labor, but actual hours matter substantially. Marketing, phone/software, insurance, permits, equipment allocation, waste, refunds, and taxes would still be deducted afterward.

## PRPD Management Targets

These are internal operating targets inferred from PRPD's model and external restaurant benchmarks. They are not universal industry standards.

| Metric | Healthy working target | Review threshold |
|---|---:|---:|
| Food cost / revenue | 25-30% | Investigate above 33% |
| Packaging and direct consumables / revenue | 3-5% | Investigate above 6% |
| Food + packaging + delivery fuel / revenue | At or below 35% | Reprice or redesign above 38% |
| Waste and unsold food / food purchased | At or below 3% | Investigate above 5% |
| Food + economically valued labor / revenue | At or below 60-65% | Growth becomes difficult above 65% |
| Fully loaded pre-tax margin after stabilization | Target at least 10-15% | Investigate below 8% |

PRPD currently passes the first three tests. Waste, labor, and fully loaded profit remain unconfirmed.

## What Must Be Recorded to Calculate True Profit

For every batch, record:

- Final booked revenue, discounts, refunds, and money actually collected
- Ingredient and packaging quantities consumed, not merely purchased
- Beginning and ending inventory for high-value proteins, dairy, sauces, desserts, and packaging
- Prep, cook, packing, cleaning, administration, customer-service, and delivery hours
- Advertising spend attributable to the ordering cycle
- Actual delivery mileage and fuel price
- Waste, failed recipes, missing meals, replacements, and owner-consumed extras
- Recurring phone, software, insurance, permits, label, and kitchen costs allocated to the batch
- Reusable equipment separately as an asset or multi-batch expense, not one-week food cost

The fully loaded formula is:

`Pre-tax profit = collected revenue - food used - packaging used - delivery - paid or imputed labor - marketing - allocated overhead - waste/refunds`

`Pre-tax margin = pre-tax profit / collected revenue`

## Current Decision

Do not reduce portions or raise every price based only on Batch 3. Current direct economics are sound. The next priority is to collect two to three weeks of actual labor, waste, inventory, and marketing data. Reprice only dishes that repeatedly exceed their direct-cost threshold or create disproportionate labor, packaging, or waste.
