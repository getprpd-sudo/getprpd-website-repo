### Chocolate Peanut Butter: One Development Batch

- 275 g ultra-filtered fat-free milk
- 30 g chocolate whey protein
- 12 g powdered peanut butter
- 5 g unsweetened cocoa
- 1 g vanilla extract
- 0.2 g fine salt
- Sweetener only if needed; record exact product and grams

Starting formula weight before blender loss: **323.2 g plus any recorded sweetener**.

Use **powdered peanut butter only** for this controlled pilot. Do not substitute
ordinary creamy peanut butter: the extra fat changes the nutrition, cost,
viscosity, cap residue, and separation behavior. Powdered peanut butter and cocoa
still contain suspended solids, so a light settled layer is expected without a
stabilizer. The bottle passes only if five normal hand shakes fully recombine it
without a gritty bottom layer.

### Strawberry Vanilla: One Development Batch

- 220 g ultra-filtered fat-free milk
- 40 g thick nonfat Greek yogurt
- 30 g vanilla whey protein
- 75 g raw strawberries, cooked and strained to exactly 50 g finished reduction
- 1 g vanilla extract
- 2 g lemon juice
- Sweetener only if needed; record exact product and grams

Expected formula weight after the strawberry reduction and before blender loss: **343 g plus any recorded sweetener**.

Strain the finished strawberry reduction through a fine-mesh sieve before
weighing the controlled 50 g input. The 2 g lemon juice is provisional: if the
first bottle shows curd-like flecks or a grainy protein layer, repeat a control
without lemon before changing any other ingredient. Do not add extra lemon to
brighten flavor because additional acid can change the dairy-protein texture.

### Test Method

1. Chill ingredients and blender vessel.
2. Record empty/tared blender-vessel weight and the exact ingredient weights used.
3. Add milk first. For Chocolate Peanut Butter, blend about 50 g of the measured
   milk with the powdered peanut butter and cocoa just until no dry pockets
   remain, then add the remaining measured milk and whey. For Strawberry
   Vanilla, add milk, yogurt, whey, the strained reduction, vanilla, and the
   exact provisional lemon amount in that order.
4. Blend only until visually smooth. Do not add water, ordinary peanut butter,
   or xanthan gum, and do not keep blending merely to eliminate foam.
5. Refrigerate for five minutes so foam can settle.
6. Record finished formula weight, measurable fluid volume, foam height, blender loss, sweetness, thickness, grit, and immediate flavor score.
7. If the actual pilot bottles have not arrived, divide each flavor into clean sealed refrigerated test containers. Mark them `R&D - NOT FOR SALE` and hold at 41 F or below.
8. Recheck at 24, 48, 72, and96 hours for aroma, separation, oxidation,
   pourability, grit, and whether five ordinary shakes recombine it. Record the
   settled-layer depth in millimeters before shaking. Fail a formula if it has a
   compact bottom sludge, persistent curd-like flecks, an off aroma, cap
   pressure, leakage, or sediment that does not disappear after five shakes.

Do not finalize calories, protein, a `12 FL OZ (355 mL)` statement, or use-through dates from this taste test. Those require exact package labels, actual filled-bottle volume, and the bottle hold test.

## Bottle And Seal Test After Delivery

Test at least three bottles per flavor:

1. Sanitize, air-dry, and inspect bottles and caps.
2. Fill cold, preserving measured headspace; record net fill weight and actual fluid volume.
3. Apply the cap evenly and record whether the tamper band seats below the neck bead.
4. Confirm first opening visibly breaks the attached ring.
5. Run upright, side, inverted, and vigorous-shake leak checks immediately and again after 24 hours refrigerated.
6. Put one capped water-filled control bottle through the normal delivery bag/route handling test.
7. Reject the package if any cap backs off, ring arrives broken, bottle deforms, or liquid reaches the threads.
