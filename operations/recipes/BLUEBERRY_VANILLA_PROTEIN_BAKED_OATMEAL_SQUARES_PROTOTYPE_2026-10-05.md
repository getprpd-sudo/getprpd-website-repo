# Blueberry Vanilla Protein Baked Oatmeal Squares

Prototype prepared October 5, 2026 and structurally revised on October 7 after the owner requested a less powder-forward, more conventional baked-oatmeal formula. The revised formula is the controlled Batch 14 test build. Nutrition and ingredient data are calculated, but the pan still requires its physical bake, cut, reheat and hold checks before the texture is described as proven.

## Product definition

A soft, sliceable blueberry-vanilla baked oatmeal breakfast, not a dry granola bar. The finished square should show visible rolled oats and blueberries, hold together when cold or reheated, and remain moist without a separate syrup or sauce cup.

Production uses one standardized square. Lean receives two squares and Bulk receives three. This avoids separate Lean and Bulk batters.

## Eight-square test pan

Use one parchment-lined 9 x 13-inch pan. Cut the finished chilled slab into two rows and four columns. The nominal footprint is approximately 3.25 x 4.5 inches per square before allowing for the pan's rounded or sloped edges. Expected thickness is approximately 3/4 to 1 inch, but the physical test must record it.

| Ingredient | Test-pan amount |
|---|---:|
| Old-fashioned rolled oats | 220 g |
| Vanilla protein powder | 48 g |
| Liquid egg whites | 220 g |
| Whole egg | 1 large |
| Fairlife fat-free milk | 180 g |
| FAGE Total 0% Greek yogurt | 160 g |
| Mashed banana | 120 g |
| Blueberries, fresh or frozen | 200 g |
| Honey | 24 g |
| Unsalted butter, melted and cooled | 10 g |
| Baking powder | 4 g |
| Vanilla extract | 8 g |
| Ground cinnamon | 2 g |
| Table salt | 2 g |

The banana is a moisture and binding ingredient. At 15 g per square it should not turn this into a banana-flavored item.

## Proposed method

1. Heat the oven to 350 F. Line the pan with parchment and lightly coat the exposed sides.
2. Whisk the oats, protein powder, baking powder, cinnamon and salt until uniform.
3. Whisk the egg whites, whole egg, milk, yogurt, mashed banana, honey, melted butter and vanilla separately until smooth.
4. Fold the wet mixture into the dry mixture. Fold in most of the blueberries last. Do not overmix.
5. Spread into an even layer in the pan. Scatter the reserved blueberries across the top and press them lightly into the batter. Record the exact raw batter weight.
6. Bake until the center is set and the edges are lightly browned. Begin checking at 28 minutes. Record the actual time and center temperature.
7. Cool completely, then chill before cutting. Record the finished slab weight and divide it into eight equal-weight squares.
8. Reheat one square from refrigerated storage and one from frozen storage. Check the center texture, edge dryness, blueberry weeping and package moisture.

## Why this version fits PRPD

- It creates a new baked-oat format without buying a waffle maker.
- One batter and one standardized cut serve both tiers.
- It uses current recurring ingredients: oats, egg whites, a whole egg, Fairlife milk, Greek yogurt, banana and blueberries.
- Protein powder is 6 g per square, 12 g in Lean and 18 g in Bulk. The texture and most of the meal's protein do not depend on a large powder load.
- It avoids an extra syrup or yogurt cup and the related filling, sealing and labeling work.
- Two Lean squares should look like a full breakfast rather than a small snack bar.
- Lean receives two medium breakfast-cake pieces, not two bite-size squares. Bulk receives three.

## Pack-out controls and continuing quality checks

- Confirm the 9 x 13-inch pan depth is appropriate and the center sets without dry edges.
- Record raw batter weight, finished slab weight and eight equal square weights.
- Confirm clean cuts after chilling.
- Confirm refrigerated texture at 24, 48 and 72 hours.
- Confirm a practical customer reheat instruction.
- Confirm whether frozen blueberries create excess surface water.
- Use the generated owner-approved estimated Lean and Bulk net weights for Batch 14 labels. Still record the actual baked slab and cut weights for future recalibration.

## Source and adaptation note

This is a PRPD-developed high-protein adaptation, not a verbatim published recipe. The revised structure is informed most directly by Skinnytaste's Baked Oatmeal with Blueberries and Bananas, rated 4.78 from 336 votes, and Sally's Baking Addiction's portable baked oatmeal cups, rated 4.8 from 24 reviews. Both use rolled oats with egg-based binding and fruit in a make-ahead format. PRPD retains only 48 g protein powder across eight squares, adds one whole egg, increases oats, reduces the original liquid load, and uses modest honey and butter for flavor and tenderness. Because the exact adaptation is new, it still requires the documented physical test.
