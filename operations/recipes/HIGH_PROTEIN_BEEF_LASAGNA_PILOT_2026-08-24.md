# PRPD High-Protein Beef Lasagna Pilot

Status: Owner-selected R&D recipe. Test before public nutrition, labels, costing, groceries, or production are locked.

## Decision

Use a traditional Italian-American ricotta filling rather than cottage cheese or a white sauce. Ricotta remains a separate cheese layer; it is not mixed into the meat sauce. This original pilot used PRPD's standard halal ground beef, cooked to 160 F and thoroughly drained, with measured part-skim mozzarella for melt and cheese pull. See the Batch 11 substitution below before shopping or cooking the current batch.

This formula intentionally does not use cottage cheese, protein pasta, cream, butter, or added cooking oil.

## One Deep 9 x 13-Inch Test Pan

Target yield: 8 Lean portions **or** 6 Bulk portions. Do not cut one pan into both sizes during the first test.

### Meat sauce

- 1,134 g / 2.5 lb raw standard halal ground beef
- 120 g yellow onion, finely diced
- 16 g fresh garlic, minced
- 1,360 g canned crushed tomatoes (or petite-diced tomatoes blended completely smooth)
- 85 g tomato paste
- 120 g water, used to rinse the tomato cans and hydrate the oven-ready noodles
- 6 g Italian seasoning
- 2 g black pepper
- Salt only after tasting the purchased sauce; record any amount used

### Ricotta filling and layers

- 425 g / 15 oz part-skim ricotta
- 2 large eggs, about 100 g edible weight
- 56 g finely grated Parmesan
- 15 g fresh parsley, chopped
- 226 g / 8 oz low-moisture part-skim mozzarella, divided
- 12 Barilla-style oven-ready lasagna sheets, approximately 204 g total

## Prep-Day Method

1. Brown the beef with the onion and garlic until the beef reaches 160 F. Break it into fine, even crumbles, then drain the rendered fat thoroughly before adding the house tomato-sauce ingredients. Do not add oil.
2. Stir in the crushed tomatoes, tomato paste, water, Italian seasoning, and black pepper. Simmer uncovered for 10 to 15 minutes, stirring often. The house sauce must remain loose enough to hydrate every noodle edge; do not reduce it into a thick paste. Taste before adding any salt.
3. Cool the meat sauce promptly in a shallow container before cold assembly. Do not place a deep pan of steaming-hot lasagna directly into the refrigerator.
4. Mix the ricotta, eggs, Parmesan, parsley, and 113 g of the mozzarella until even. Reserve the other 113 g mozzarella for the top.
5. Lightly coat a deep 9 x 13-inch pan with cooking spray. Spread a thin layer of meat sauce over the bottom.
6. Add 3 oven-ready sheets and cover every dry edge with sauce. Add one-third of the ricotta filling and an even layer of meat sauce.
7. Repeat until there are 4 noodle layers. Finish the final noodle layer with the remaining meat sauce and the reserved mozzarella.
8. Cover tightly and refrigerate up to 24 hours.

## Cook-Day Method

1. Bake the refrigerated, covered pan at 375 F for 55 to 60 minutes.
2. Remove the cover and bake another 10 to 15 minutes until bubbling and lightly browned.
3. Verify at least 165 F in the center and confirm that a knife passes easily through the noodles.
4. Rest for 15 minutes before cutting. The rest is required for clean portions.
5. Record the finished pan weight after the rest, excluding the pan. Cut into 8 equal Lean portions or 6 equal Bulk portions and verify each portion by weight.

## How This Differs From a Conventional Ricotta Lasagna

- Uses 2.5 lb of standard ground beef, cooked to 160 F and thoroughly drained, instead of a sausage-heavy meat layer.
- Uses part-skim ricotta and part-skim mozzarella.
- Uses 8 oz mozzarella rather than the 16 oz used in Barilla's richer three-layer recipe.
- Uses no cream, butter, bechamel, or added oil.
- Keeps real noodles rather than replacing them with vegetables, preserving the expected lasagna experience.
- Measures the entire pan and every final portion instead of relying only on visual cuts.

## Test Gates

- Confirm the exact purchased package panels before any customer-facing macro or label is approved.
- Confirm the deep pan does not overflow with the 2.5 lb meat formula.
- Confirm all 12 no-boil sheets soften completely after refrigerated assembly.
- Photograph the full pan, top browning, side profile, and one plated cross-section.
- Record finished pan weight, edge loss, Lean cut weights, and one reheated portion weight.
- Reheat one refrigerated portion to 165 F and assess moisture, noodle texture, ricotta texture, and cheese pull.
- Freeze and reheat one portion to confirm that the ricotta layer does not become watery or grainy.
- Do not publish or print labels until the test passes.

## Batch 11 owner-approved single-cheese substitution, September 17

The eight currently ordered lasagnas use the same H-E-B fat-free shredded mozzarella as the other Batch 11 dishes, 226 g per full pilot-pan formula, instead of a separate part-skim mozzarella purchase. The current controlled recipe and planner also use the one consolidated onion pull. Retain the 425 g part-skim ricotta layer; it is a different cheese with a different purpose. The revised calculated menu estimates are 530 calories and 51 g protein for Lean, and 710 calories and 67 g protein for Bulk. Check melt, moisture, and the purchased cheese panel before releasing final labels. The original part-skim test formula and estimates above remain historical, not the Batch 11 shopping instruction.

## Batch 11 actual-build observation, September 21, 2026

The owner confirmed **24 oven-ready sheets total, 12 per tray, three sheets per layer**. This describes four noodle layers per tray. The owner then explicitly confirmed that **no extra meat, sauce, ricotta, mozzarella or other ingredient was added**. Only the noodles increased.

For six Lean plus two Bulk orders, the controlled formula multiplier is `6/8 + 2/6 = 13/12`, so the listed noodle requirement was 13 sheets. Actual use was 11 sheets higher. This is a noodle/layout deviation, not evidence that the whole formula should be doubled. At the working 17 g/sheet assumption it represents 187 g extra dry pasta, but actual purchased sheet weight was not measured.

The owner reports using about one full tray plus three quarters of the second. Neither final edible batch weight nor individual portions was recorded. This observation cannot validate the published net weights or the old pan-capacity promise. Last batch's actual recipe differed from its nutrition calculation; do not silently treat the old nutrition as verified for the 24-sheet build.

Before the next lasagna launch: choose the standard build (the original controlled noodle amount with an appropriate layout, or the owner's higher-noodle layout), pair pan area/layer count with that formula, recalculate nutrition and purchasing if noodles change, then measure the finished yield. Keep all actuals separate from the already-completed Batch 11 production lock.
