## 1. PRPD Protein Box - Whole Mini Apple

### Decision

Test one whole **Rockit miniature apple** instead of cutting a standard apple. Rockit identifies the variety as a naturally miniature snack apple, and Walmart, Kroger, and H-E-B list three-pound packs. Retail listings describe two apples as about 70 calories, which implies roughly 65-70 g edible fruit per apple using the controlled apple nutrition reference.

### Fit and holding test

1. Buy one pack only; stock varies by store.
2. Weigh ten apples individually and record the range and average.
3. Test one 55-80 g whole apple in the divided Protein Box with two hard-boiled eggs, one beef-breakfast slice, 28 g mozzarella, 60 g cucumber, and the sealed dip.
4. Reject fruit that touches the dip lid, prevents closure, or bruises when the container is stacked.
5. Refrigerate one complete box and inspect at 24 and 72 hours.
6. If the whole mini apple passes, calculate nutrition from the measured batch average rather than the former 100 g assumption.

Do not use cut apple in the sellable box unless a separate anti-browning and 72-hour refrigerated test passes. If mini apples are unavailable, the controlled alternative is a weighed cherry-tomato component with a new description and nutrition calculation.

Sources: [Rockit miniature apples](https://www.rockitapple.com/rockit-apple/), [Walmart listing](https://www.walmart.com/ip/309762096), [Kroger listing](https://www.kroger.com/p/rockit-apples-shuttle-pack-in-clamshell/0088828940351).

## 2. Cheeseburger Hot Pocket Dough - Ten-Pocket Pilot

### September 21, 2026 owner update

The owner confirmed that later Cheeseburger Hot Pocket and Egg Bites tests both worked. Hot Pockets are a successful return candidate for Batch 12. The exact incorporated flour, yogurt, bench flour, filling, finished piece count and whether this pilot was followed unchanged were not supplied. Preserve the successful result, but do not treat the failed old formula or this proposed pilot as the measured final formula until reconciled. The historical failure and proposed test below are retained as provenance, not a claim that the latest attempt failed.

### Why the old formula failed

The old 400 g flour / 410 g yogurt batch treated a volume-style “one cup to one cup” internet formula as if it were a reliable weight formula. Yogurt thickness, flour measuring, protein content, and bench flour all change the result. A filled pocket also needs more structure than a flatbread. Established yogurt-dough methods use thick Greek yogurt, flour with baking powder, and additional flour until the dough is tacky but no longer sticky.

### Proposed controlled hydration test

- All-purpose flour: **480 g**
- Thick plain nonfat Greek yogurt: **360 g to start, plus a separate 50 g reserve**
- Baking powder: **12 g**
- Fine salt: **8 g**
- Separate weighed bench flour: **20 g maximum**, used only after the yogurt reserve is controlled
- Egg wash: weigh separately; not part of the dough

Baker's percentages before bench flour: flour 100%, yogurt begins at 75% and may rise to 85.4%, baking powder 2.5%, salt 1.7%. The flour:yogurt ratio therefore stays between 1.33:1 and 1.17:1 by weight rather than blindly repeating the failed near-1:1 batch. Published yogurt-dough recipes range from 1:1 by weight to roughly 1.5 cups flour per cup yogurt; that spread is exactly why PRPD must add the wet ingredient gradually and record the actual incorporated grams.

### Method

1. Whisk 480 g flour, 12 g baking powder, and 8 g salt until uniform.
2. Add **360 g** stirred, cold Greek yogurt. Mix until shaggy, then add the separate reserve in **10 g additions only when dry flour remains**. Stop as soon as the dough comes together; do not assume all 410 g belongs in the bowl.
3. Rest covered for 10 minutes so the flour hydrates before judging stickiness.
4. Knead briefly. The target is soft and tacky but it must release cleanly from a dry finger. If the full yogurt reserve is incorporated and the dough is still sticky after the rest, add the separate bench flour only in weighed 5 g additions.
5. Record incorporated yogurt, incorporated bench flour, and finished dough weight.
6. Divide into ten equal portions, expected around **86-91 g each** before any bench flour depending on how much of the yogurt reserve is needed.
7. Roll evenly, add fully cooked and completely cooled **dry** filling, leave a clean sealing border, crimp firmly, and vent the top.
8. Brush with weighed egg wash and bake at 400 F until browned and structurally set, beginning checks at 18 minutes.
9. Record leakage, baked weight, same-day texture, and day-three microwave/air-fryer reheat quality.

### Pass gate

- Ten complete pockets with no emergency unweighed flour.
- Dough rolls without tearing or sticking.
- No wet seam failures or gummy interior.
- Finished dough grams per pocket are repeatable.
- Day-three reheating remains acceptable.

Sources: [Illawarra Shoalhaven Greek-yogurt dough guidance](https://www.islhd.health.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/Health_Promotion/Recipes/Healthy_dough_for_pizza_and_scrolls1.pdf), [NDSU yogurt hand-pie method](https://www.ndsu.edu/agriculture/extension/recipes/whole-wheat-wojapi-and-cinnamon-hand-pies).

## 3. Baked Strawberry-Lemon Protein Cheesecake Squares - Eight-Square Pilot

### Direction

Retire both the loose former cup and the proposed Biscoff crunch cup. This is a genuinely different dessert format: a crustless baked cheesecake slab cut into individual squares. It uses no Biscoff, no cookie crumb layer, and no parfait-style assembly.

### Proposed 8-inch pan formula

- Reduced-fat cream cheese, softened: **340 g**
- 2% cottage cheese, blended completely smooth: **300 g**
- Thick nonfat Greek yogurt: **100 g**
- Large eggs: **100 g out of shell**
- Vanilla whey protein powder: **40 g**
- Cornstarch: **18 g**
- Vanilla extract: **4 g**
- Lemon juice: **12 g**
- Fine salt: **1 g**
- Recorded approved sweetener: start with the package-equivalent sweetness of **60 g sugar**, then taste the unbaked base only if all eggs are pasteurized; otherwise bake a teaspoon-sized tester

#### Strawberry swirl

- Strawberries: **250 g**
- Lemon juice: **6 g**
- Cornstarch: **5 g**
- Recorded sugar or approved sweetener: start at **15 g sugar equivalent**

Cook the strawberry mixture until thick and reduced to a recorded finished weight near **120-140 g**. Cool it completely before swirling it into the cheesecake base.

### Method

1. Line an 8-inch square metal pan with parchment. Heat the oven to **325 F**.
2. Blend the cottage cheese completely smooth. Beat the cream cheese separately until lump-free, then mix in the cottage cheese and yogurt.
3. Mix in the weighed eggs, whey, cornstarch, vanilla, lemon, salt, and sweetener on low speed. Do not whip excess air into the batter.
4. Pour into the lined pan. Drop the fully cooled strawberry reduction over the surface and make a shallow swirl; do not mix it throughout.
5. Bake until the outside is set and the center still moves slightly as one piece, beginning checks at **32 minutes**. Record time and center temperature; do not use time alone as the permanent control.
6. Cool no more than two hours at room temperature, then refrigerate until completely set, preferably overnight.
7. Cut into eight equal weighed squares. Package in parchment-lined individual dessert containers rather than cups.

### Pass gate

- Clear baked-cheesecake texture, not yogurt mousse or pudding.
- A square lifts cleanly and retains its shape after 30 minutes out of the refrigerator.
- Strawberry flavor is distinct and the swirl does not weep at 24 or 72 hours.
- No chalky whey flavor and no rubbery egg texture.
- Eight repeatable finished weights with a photographed package-label set for every ingredient.

Exact nutrition, allergens, price, and a customer-facing name remain blocked until the physical test passes. The controlled baking direction is based on a bar-style cheesecake method in which the filling is baked until set but still soft in the center; PRPD's high-protein substitutions require its own physical validation. Sources: [King Arthur cheesecake bars](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/recipes/vanilla-bean-cheesecake-bars-recipe), [University of Minnesota perishable-dessert cold-holding guidance](https://extension.umn.edu/food-safety-basics/safe-food-temperatures).

## 4. One Universal Refrigerator-Pickle System

### Decision

Do not buy an inconsistent mixed-pickle product and do not put wet pickles inside Hot Pocket filling. If an approved dish needs acidic crunch, test one refrigerated house pickle and pack it drained in a separate portion.

### Extension-tested base ratio

For 3.5-4 cups prepared vegetables:

- **1 cup 5% acidity vinegar**
- **1 cup water**
- **1 tablespoon pickling salt**
- **1 tablespoon sugar** to start; the tested source allows 1-2 tablespoons
- Optional whole/fresh seasonings such as garlic, dill, jalapeno, peppercorn, or cumin seed

Recommended first vegetable mix: thin red onion and carrot with a small amount of jalapeno. This holds more cleanly than a tomato-based salad and avoids putting wet cucumber into a baked pocket.

1. Wash and uniformly cut the vegetables.
2. Heat vinegar, water, salt, and sugar to a simmer; dissolve completely.
3. Pour over vegetables in a sanitized food-safe container.
4. Refrigerate immediately and allow 1-3 days for flavor development.
5. For PRPD, set a conservative seven-day operational use limit even though the extension recipe permits longer refrigerated storage.
6. Drain and weigh a 30 g serving before packing. Keep it separate from hot food.
7. Never reuse brine that has already held vegetables.

The vinegar must be labeled 5% acidity. Do not reduce the vinegar or increase the water.

Sources: [Oregon State refrigerator-pickled vegetables](https://extension.oregonstate.edu/imported-publication/refrigerator-pickled-vegetables), [University of Minnesota pickling safety](https://extension.umn.edu/preserve-your-own-food/pickling).

## 6. Strawberry Protein Overnight Oats - One 12 oz Grab & Go Cup

### Corrected single-size pilot

This product has **one Grab & Go size only**. It is not a Lean/Bulk recipe. The former draft was too large for the normal 12 oz cup and is retired.

- Old-fashioned rolled oats: **40 g**
- FAGE Total 0% Greek yogurt: **120 g**
- Fairlife fat-free milk: **75 g**
- Premier Protein vanilla powder: **15 g**
- Raw strawberries: **45 g**, cooked down to a recorded **30 g finished reduction**
- Chia seeds: **5 g**
- Honey: **7 g**
- Vanilla extract: **1 g**
- Fine salt: **0.2 g**

The weighed input is **308.2 g**. After the strawberry reduction loses about 15 g of water, the estimated packaged fill is **293.2 g**. That is a planning target, not a guaranteed cup fit.

### Development nutrition estimate

Using the controlled PRPD ingredient profiles, one cup is approximately **360 calories, 35 g protein, 45 g carbohydrate, 7 g fiber, and 5 g fat**. Expected allergen: **milk**. Confirm the Premier Protein and oat package labels before printing because the powder may declare soy and the oats may carry wheat/gluten cross-contact language.

### Mixing and package test

1. Cook 45 g strawberries to **30 g finished reduction**, then cool completely.
2. Whisk milk, yogurt, whey, honey, vanilla, and salt until no protein-powder pockets remain.
3. Fold in oats and chia. Pack the base, then add the cooled strawberry reduction.
4. Record the exact filled cup weight and confirm the lid closes without the product touching or bowing it.
5. Chill overnight and inspect at 12, 24, 48, and 72 hours for overflow, dry texture, liquid separation, whey grit, and strawberry weeping.
6. If the oats are too thick, adjust only the next controlled test in 5 g milk increments. Do not add unrecorded liquid to sellable cups.

### Pass gate

- Fits the normal 12 oz cup with safe headspace.
- Reaches a spoonable overnight-oat texture through 72 hours.
- No watery strawberry layer or dry center.
- Final filled weight is repeatable within 5 g across three test cups.
- Exact package labels, final yield, allergens, nutrition, price, and label fit are recorded before sale.

## 7. Harissa Honey Chicken - Four-Meal Pilot

### Proposed menu format

Boneless halal chicken thigh with a measured harissa-honey glaze, basmati rice, and roasted broccoli. The glaze is incorporated into the chicken; do not add an automatic side cup.

### Controlled one-kilogram chicken test

- Boneless skinless chicken thigh, raw: **1,000 g**
- Harissa paste: **60 g**
- Honey: **40 g**
- Tomato paste: **30 g**
- Lemon juice: **30 g**
- Fresh garlic: **15 g**
- Avocado oil: **20 g**
- Smoked paprika: **3 g**
- Ground coriander: **3 g**
- Fine salt: **8 g**

This is a PRPD pilot—not a production formula. It uses the common harissa/honey/garlic structure shown by established harissa recipes, while keeping the oil and honey controlled for meal-prep nutrition. New York Shuk uses harissa, honey, garlic, and salt on chicken; the American Culinary Federation version also uses tomato paste, lemon, oil, coriander, and smoked paprika.

### Proposed plated structure

- Lean: begin testing at **200 g raw chicken allocation, 55 g dry rice, and 100 g broccoli**.
- Bulk: begin testing at **300 g raw chicken allocation, 70 g dry rice, and 130 g broccoli**.

The final plated grams and nutrition remain blocked until the chicken, glaze, rice, and broccoli yields are recorded.

### Method

1. Photograph and record the exact harissa, honey, tomato-paste, and seasoning package labels.
2. Mix the entire glaze and record its starting weight.
3. Reserve no raw-chicken-contact glaze for finishing. If a finishing glaze is desired, divide and label it before the chicken touches the main batch.
4. Coat the chicken and marinate refrigerated. Record the incorporated marinade weight.
5. Cook within the continuous chicken block to a recorded endpoint of at least **165 F**. Record cooked chicken weight before any clean finishing glaze.
6. Add only the recorded clean finishing glaze, if used, then record the final glazed yield.
7. Plate with the measured rice and broccoli builds and perform same-day and day-three taste/reheat checks.

### Pass gate

- Sweet heat is distinct but not harsher than the existing PRPD menu.
- Glaze clings after reheating without pooling or burning.
- Broccoli and rice remain good through day three.
- Lean and Bulk chicken, rice, and broccoli portions are repeatable.
- Exact brand nutrition, cooked yields, allergens, price, and label statement are regenerated before publication.

Sources: [New York Shuk Harissa-Honey Chicken](https://www.nyshuk.com/harissa-honey-chicken-wings), [American Culinary Federation harissa guide and recipe](https://www.acfchefs.org/Downloads/IOTM/202509-Handout.pdf).
