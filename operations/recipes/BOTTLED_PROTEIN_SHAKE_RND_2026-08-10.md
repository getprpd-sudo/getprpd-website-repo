# Bottled Protein Shake R&D Plan

Date: August 10, 2026
Status: **FOLLOWING-BATCH DEVELOPMENT ONLY / NOT APPROVED FOR SALE**

The first launch should be two flavors, not a large beverage menu. These are refrigerated prepared beverages, not shelf-stable shakes.

## Packaging Direction

- Preferred test package: **12 fl oz / 355 mL clear food-grade PET round bullet beverage bottle with a matching 38 mm 358DBJ tamper-evident neck and cap**. The current candidate lists a 2.343-inch-high label panel and 8.3566-inch full-wrap width.
- Buy the bottle and its exact matching breakaway-band cap as one system. Do not mix caps and bottles that merely look the same; the neck finish must match.
- Fill cold, cap immediately, and confirm the plastic ring remains attached until first opening. This initial system does **not** require a heat gun or induction sealer.
- Run upright, side, inverted, vigorous-shake, and simulated-delivery leak checks at filling and after 24 hours refrigerated.
- A matched induction liner and sealer can be evaluated later, but only as a supplier-approved bottle/cap/liner system. A generic foil or adhesive seal is not acceptable.
- Use a condensation-resistant **BOPP or waterproof polyester label with a permanent cold-temperature adhesive**. Apply it to a clean, dry, room-temperature bottle before chilling; ordinary paper labels will wrinkle and smear.
- For the cited candidate bottle, begin only with a paper fit mockup no larger than approximately **2.1 inches high by 8.0 inches wide**, then resize from the physical sample. Do not order printed rolls from drawing dimensions alone.
- Do not print permanent labels until actual filled volume and density prove the net-quantity statement.

## Flavor Pilot A - Chocolate Peanut Butter

Development starting point for one bottle:

- Ultra-filtered fat-free milk: **275 g**
- Chocolate whey protein: **30 g**
- Powdered peanut butter: **12 g**
- Unsweetened cocoa: **5 g**
- Vanilla extract: **1 g**
- Fine salt: **0.2 g**
- Recorded approved sweetener, only if needed

Ingredient weight before blender loss: **323.2 g**.

This formula contains no water; milk is the only liquid base. The estimated macros belong to the listed formula, **not yet to a verified 12 fl oz filled bottle**. During the physical test, measure the blended volume and add only the exact purchased milk—not water—if a small adjustment is needed to reach the controlled final batch volume. Recalculate macros from the recorded added milk and filled yield.

Development nutrition estimate: approximately **265 calories, 44 g protein, 16 g carbohydrate, 4 g fiber, and 4 g fat**.

Allergens expected: **milk, peanut**; the exact Premier Protein package may also declare **soy**. The final Nutrition Facts and allergen line must come from the purchased packages, not this development estimate.

## Flavor Pilot B - Strawberry Vanilla

Development starting point for one bottle:

- Ultra-filtered fat-free milk: **220 g**
- Thick nonfat Greek yogurt: **40 g**
- Vanilla whey protein: **30 g**
- Raw strawberries: **75 g**, cooked and strained to a recorded **50 g finished reduction**
- Vanilla extract: **1 g**
- Lemon juice: **2 g**
- Recorded approved sweetener, only if needed

Raw ingredient input is **368 g**. After reducing the strawberries by 25 g, the estimated formula weight is **343 g before blender loss**.

This formula also contains no water. Its estimated macros belong to the listed formula, not yet to a verified 12 fl oz fill. Any volume adjustment must use the exact purchased milk and must be recorded before nutrition is finalized.

Development nutrition estimate: approximately **240 calories, 40 g protein, 15 g carbohydrate, 2 g fiber, and 2 g fat**.

Allergen expected: **milk**; the exact Premier Protein package may also declare **soy**. The final Nutrition Facts and allergen line must come from the purchased packages, not this development estimate.

These weights are formulation tests, not a finished net-volume claim. Record the blender loss and actual filled-bottle weight.

## Mixing Method

Xanthan gum is intentionally excluded by owner decision. Natural separation is acceptable; the bottle must say **SHAKE WELL** and the 72-hour test must confirm that ordinary shaking recombines the drink acceptably.

1. Chill all ingredients and the sanitized blender vessel before use.
2. Add milk first, then yogurt if used, protein powder, flavor ingredients, and salt.
3. Blend only until smooth to limit foam. Let the shake stand refrigerated for five minutes, then record total formula weight and foam height.
4. Fill the sanitized bottle to the physically validated 12 fl oz line while preserving cap headspace. Record filled net weight and remaining blender loss.
5. Cap immediately with the exact tamper-evident closure, wipe the exterior, label, and refrigerate at 41 F or below.

## Label System

The bottle label needs four information zones:

1. **Front:** PRPD, flavor, `KEEP REFRIGERATED`, and the validated protein claim.
2. **Nutrition panel:** complete Nutrition Facts based on the exact packages and finished recipe yield.
3. **Ingredients and business panel:** ingredients and subingredients in descending order by weight, `CONTAINS` statement, PRPD business name/address statement, and storage directions.
4. **Variable production panel:** `MADE`, `USE THROUGH`, batch ID, and `SHAKE WELL`. A separate small waterproof variable-data sticker is acceptable for dates/batch if the main wrap is preprinted.

Use `12 FL OZ (355 mL)` in the bottom third only after the physical fill test proves that every bottle contains at least that fluid volume. Grams alone cannot establish fluid ounces because shake density varies.

Proposed handling copy: **KEEP REFRIGERATED AT 41 F OR BELOW - SHAKE WELL - USE WITHIN 24 HOURS OF OPENING.** Start with a conservative 72-hour made/use-through window until PRPD's hold test and regulator guidance support anything longer.

Because a prominent `44 g Protein` or `40 g Protein` statement is a nutrient-content claim, the launch label should include a complete Nutrition Facts panel even if a small-business exemption might otherwise be available.

## Required Physical Tests

1. Photograph every ingredient and package Nutrition/Allergen label.
2. Blend one bottle of each flavor, record total batch weight and filled weight, and confirm the bottle closes with safe headspace.
3. Refrigerate continuously at 41 F or below.
4. Inspect and taste at 0, 24, 48, and 72 hours for separation, gumminess, whey grit, strawberry oxidation, off aroma, cap leakage, and shake-to-recombine performance.
5. Record actual pH as quality information only; do not use it to claim shelf stability.
6. Pass only if both flavors remain safe-looking, pleasant, pourable, and readily recombined through 72 hours.
7. Use a conservative made/use-through rule supported by the physical test and regulator guidance. The FDA Food Code's seven-day date-marking ceiling for refrigerated ready-to-eat TCS food is not proof that a homemade dairy shake retains acceptable quality for seven days.
8. Calculate nutrition and cost from exact brands, actual finished yield, bottle/cap/label cost, and test fill.
9. Confirm that ordinary shaking recombines any natural separation at 0, 24, 48, and 72 hours.

## Launch Recommendation

- Two flavors only.
- Small initial quantity cap, such as six bottles per flavor.
- Keep refrigerated and deliver only inside the existing cold chain.
- Do not promise shelf stability, meal replacement, muscle gain, recovery, or medical outcomes.
- Add to Grab & Go only after regulatory coverage, the 72-hour test, exact labels, bottle fit, and margins pass.
