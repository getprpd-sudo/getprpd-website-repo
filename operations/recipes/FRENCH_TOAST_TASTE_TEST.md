# French Toast - Taste-First Test

Status: Fresh taste and three-slice Lean production yield approved July 18, 2026; refrigerated reheat check pending

## Confirmed Finished Builds

| Tier | Toast | Sides |
|---|---:|---|
| Lean | 3 slices | Strawberries, banana, and whipped cream |
| Bulk | 4 slices | Strawberries, banana, and whipped cream |

## Goal

Make French toast that customers would order because it tastes good, not merely because it has high protein. Whole eggs, real sugar, butter, fruit, and whipped cream may remain when they materially improve flavor and texture.

The preferred Lean range is roughly 400-500 calories and 30-40g protein, but taste decides the final formula. Bulk should be the same recipe scaled to four slices without forcing an excessive amount of protein powder or egg whites into the custard.

## Starting Test Formula - 12 Slices

This is a prototype, not the final recipe. Blend the custard smooth before tasting or calculating it.

- 12 slices bread; record the brand, package macros, and total bread weight
- 4 whole eggs
- 300g egg whites
- 300g cottage cheese
- 160g milk
- 1 scoop protein powder total across all 12 slices
- 24g brown sugar
- 2 teaspoons vanilla
- 2 teaspoons cinnamon
- Pinch of salt
- Start with 20g butter for cooking the full batch and record any additional butter used

This gives each three-slice Lean serving approximately one whole egg, 75g egg whites, 75g cottage cheese, one-quarter scoop of protein, and 6g sugar before accounting for normal shared-batch variation.

## One Lean Test - Make This Today

- Bread: 3 slices
- Whole egg: 1 large
- Egg whites: 75g
- Cottage cheese: 75g
- Milk: 40g
- Vanilla protein powder: 8.7g
- Brown sugar: 12g for the next test; the 6g test tasted excellent but needed more sweetness
- Vanilla extract: 2.5g
- Ground cinnamon: 1.3g
- Fine salt: small pinch
- Butter for cooking: start with 5g and record actual use

Blend the custard completely smooth. This is the direct one-quarter scale of the 12-slice prototype, so no large batch is required for the first taste decision.

## July 14 Kitchen Result

- The 6g-sugar version tasted excellent fresh and had no dry bread or excess batter at the intended point.
- Actual yield was four fully coated 80-calorie bread slices plus a fifth slice that was only partially coated. Treat this as a four-slice controlled yield, not five complete slices.
- The custard quantity is therefore a natural starting unit for one four-slice Bulk meal. Production scaling for three-slice Lean meals should be based on the observed four-slice yield rather than the earlier 12-slice assumption.
- Increase brown sugar from 6g to 12g in the full four-slice custard unit. This adds only 24 calories to the entire unit compared with the first test.
- Serve measured sugar-free maple syrup separately. Record its brand and grams per cup before calculating final macros.
- Refrigerated and reheated texture remains unverified.

## July 18 Production Result

- The generated Lean batch soaked exactly three slices.
- No meaningful shortage or excess batter was reported.
- Taste passed again.
- Keep the three-slice Lean build. The remaining useful validation is refrigerated storage and reheating quality, not another fresh-yield change.

Do not add more protein powder unless the test still tastes like normal French toast. If it tastes dry, rubbery, chalky, or too egg-white-heavy, reduce egg whites or protein powder before changing the sugar, whole egg, or butter.

## Sides Per Meal - Starting Point

- Strawberries: 50g
- Banana: 40g
- Whipped cream: 10g

Pack the fruit and whipped cream separately. Adjust these amounts based on appearance and taste, then retain the amounts selected for the final calculation.

## Test Decisions

- Is the center custardy rather than dry?
- Is any protein-powder flavor noticeable?
- Does the toast brown properly?
- Does it reheat without becoming rubbery?
- Does the fruit remain separate from the toast?
- Would PRPD serve this version even if the protein number were hidden?

Only calculate and publish the final working macros after the preferred version is selected.
