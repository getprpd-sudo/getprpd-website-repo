# Batch 13 Cheeseburger Hot Pocket dough pilot

## October 4 post-batch decision — retired as-is

The owner physically tried this Batch 13 version. He liked the dough itself, but oven-baking the full batch produced tough pockets and took too long for practical cook-day production. **Retire this exact Cheeseburger Hot Pocket recipe and workflow; do not re-offer or auto-schedule it.** The measured formula and source notes below remain a historical record, not a current release recipe or proof of a successful production method. A future faster alternative or different dish requires a separate timed, weighed, taste/reheat-tested pilot and owner approval. Customer labels from the completed batch are historical and are not being changed by this decision.

Status: **measured working adaptation, not a tested PRPD success**. The old 400 g flour / 410 g yogurt dough failed and is retired. This document changes the internal cooking and purchasing recipe only; it does not revise customer labels or published macros.

## Published source and adaptation

[King Arthur Baking's Homemade Pizza Pockets](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/recipes/homemade-pizza-pockets-recipe) makes twelve small 4-by-3-inch pockets from 360 g all-purpose flour, 227 g cold water, 57 g cold unsalted butter, 1 tablespoon sugar, 2 teaspoons instant yeast and 2 teaspoons table salt. It rests the dough chilled for 20 minutes to four hours, then bakes its small pizza pockets at 400°F for 20–25 minutes.

PRPD's cheeseburger filling is materially heavier than the source pizza filling. This pilot scales the source dough 3.5 times to make **28 larger pockets**, roughly **84 g dough per pocket** before bench flour. It reduces dough salt to about 2% of flour because the beef-and-cheese filling also contains salt. It omits the source egg wash so no new egg ingredient is introduced. These size, salt, filling, sealing, and bake-time changes are **untested PRPD adaptations**, not instructions claimed to be from King Arthur.

## Method and timing

1. **Prep Day:** Cook the existing measured PRPD beef, onion, ketchup and salt filling. Ground beef must reach **160°F** by thermometer, per [USDA FSIS](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/meat/ground-beef-and-food-safety). Cook off excess liquid, cool promptly, and hold refrigerated. Keep mozzarella measured and cold. Do not mix this dough the night before: King Arthur's chilled rest is 20 minutes to four hours.
2. **Cook Day:** Mix flour, sugar, yeast and dough salt. Rub or cut in the cold butter until crumbly with some small butter pieces, then add cold water gradually until a cohesive, slightly sticky dough forms. Chill covered for at least 20 minutes and no longer than four hours under the source method.
3. Weigh the finished dough and portion into 28 equal pieces. Roll each thin and even. Add only the measured, cold filling and cheese, keeping the seal clear. Lightly moisten edges with water, fold, crimp with a fork, and cut two small steam vents. Do not overstuff. Maintain any Tania no-pickle customization; the base recipe itself has no purchased pickle filling.
4. Bake on parchment at **400°F**. King Arthur's 20–25 minutes applies to its smaller pizza pockets, **not a verified PRPD bake time**. Watch the first load, record actual time and appearance, and check that the dough is fully baked through without a raw center. Cool and pack only after the kitchen's standard cooked-food controls are met.

Before this becomes a validated house formula, log actual bench flour, dough weights, cooked/drained beef yield, filling per pocket, pocket count, sealing or leakage, bake time, and reheat quality. If the first pocket handles or bakes poorly, hold the load and record an adjustment; do not silently alter the nutrition model or labels.
