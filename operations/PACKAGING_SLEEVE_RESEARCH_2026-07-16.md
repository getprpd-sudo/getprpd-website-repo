# PRPD Container Sleeve Research

Updated: July 16, 2026

## Recommendation

Use a **universal full-width PRPD paperboard sleeve plus a smaller variable meal-information label**.

The universal sleeve should carry the permanent brand content:

- PRPD / Meals. Prepped.
- halal, high-protein, local-DFW positioning;
- social handles and website;
- general storage and handling language;
- a reserved light information panel for the weekly meal label.

The variable label should carry the information that changes by dish, tier, and production date:

- meal name and Lean/Bulk tier;
- calories, protein, carbohydrates, fat, fiber, sodium, and ingredient/allergen text;
- made/use-by dates;
- meal-specific storage and reheating instructions.

This hybrid prevents PRPD from holding obsolete sleeves when menus, recipes, macros, or tiers change. It also allows one sleeve inventory to serve every breakfast, main, and dessert container of the same physical size.

## Physical Format

- Style: scored and glued paperboard box sleeve, open at both short ends.
- Coverage: top, both long sides, and bottom of the closed meal container.
- Suggested material for quoting: 14-18 pt SBS/white cardstock.
- Suggested finish: matte aqueous coating for light condensation resistance and a premium low-glare appearance.
- Printing: exterior only, restrained forest-green coverage to control ink and preserve the warm cream brand direction.
- Closure: pre-glued vendor sleeve where affordable; otherwise a hidden overlap secured with food-packaging-safe double-sided tape.
- The sleeve goes around the sealed container and does not contact food directly.

Do not approve a dieline until the exact external container measurements are recorded: lid length, lid width, total closed height, base taper, corner radius, and the position of any lid ridge or locking tabs. Test at least five filled containers because lid bowing and food volume can change the fit.

## Vendor Findings

- UPrinting offers custom box sleeves from quantities as low as one, 14/18/24 pt cardstock, custom sizing, and approximately 8-10 business-day standard production. Its displayed 250-piece example was approximately $415 ($1.66 each) before shipping and is too expensive for routine PRPD use without another quote.
- PrintRunner lists a 250-piece minimum and approximately six business days of standard production.
- Greenerprinter advertises short-run custom belly bands with 2-3 day production, but a belly band may not provide the full coverage PRPD wants.
- Other no-minimum custom vendors exist, but samples, dielines, material specifications, shipping cost, and independent print-quality checks are required before placing a production order.

## Practical Rollout

1. Record the exact container brand, model, and closed dimensions.
2. Build a plain-paper prototype to confirm fit and opening direction.
3. Print 5-10 cardstock prototypes locally and test condensation, stacking, refrigeration, transport, and customer opening.
4. Keep using Avery labels until the prototype passes.
5. Request comparable quotes for 250 and 500 universal sleeves.
6. Target a finished sleeve cost below approximately $0.50-$0.75 each before replacing the current label system. A higher cost must be justified by a higher selling price, stronger retention, or a premium-client package.
7. Continue using the existing Label Studio for the variable information panel; adapt its output only after the sleeve dimensions are approved.

## Timing

A professionally die-cut printed sleeve is unlikely to be production-ready for the immediately following batch because exact measurement, dieline proofing, sample fit, printing, and shipping must happen first. A local cardstock prototype can be tested next week without committing to a large order.

## Decision Gate

Do not order production sleeves until PRPD has:

- one stable container size and supplier;
- a passed filled-container fit test;
- an approved universal design;
- an approved variable-label size;
- a delivered unit-cost quote;
- confirmation that the sleeve does not interfere with stacking, refrigeration, delivery bags, or reheating removal.
