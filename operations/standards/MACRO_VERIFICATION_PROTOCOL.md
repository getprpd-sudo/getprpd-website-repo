# PRPD Working Macro Standard

Updated: July 14, 2026

## Purpose

PRPD is a growing local meal-prep business, not a nutrition laboratory. The goal is to provide customers with honest, repeatable working estimates while keeping taste and food quality first.

Calories and protein are guardrails. A recipe should not be made noticeably worse just to force a higher protein number or a lower calorie number.

## Recipe Status

- **Draft:** an idea or historical recipe that is not ready for the current menu.
- **Kitchen Tested:** cooked and tasted successfully with the actual ingredients intended for sale.
- **Current Recipe:** the recipe PRPD is presently selling. Its portion sizes and working calories/protein have been recorded.

"Current Recipe" does not mean laboratory tested. Published values remain estimates based on ingredient labels, measured ingredients, and actual portions.

## Practical Approval Standard

A recipe can become the Current Recipe when:

1. PRPD has cooked and tasted it successfully.
2. The ingredients and amounts actually used are written down.
3. Lean and Bulk portions are physically defined.
4. Calories and protein are calculated from those portions.
5. The result fits the container or wrap and reheats properly.
6. The recipe has a date and version.

Carbohydrates, fat, fiber, sodium, detailed yields, and exact brand records are useful improvements, but they should not prevent a good recipe from being used while PRPD is operating at its current scale.

## Calculation Method

- Packaged products: use the Nutrition Facts panel from the product normally purchased.
- Basic meat and produce: use the exact package label when available; otherwise use an appropriate USDA FoodData Central entry and record whether the entry is raw, cooked, drained, skinless, or bone-in.
- Batch recipes: total every ingredient's nutrients for the full batch, then allocate nutrients using the actual finished batch yield and plated component grams.
- Drained ground beef: do not treat raw 85/15 and raw 90/10 as interchangeable. Use the actual beef specification and account for discarded rendered fat.
- Rice and pasta: calculate from dry weight, then use cooked yield for portion control. Water changes weight, not the dry ingredient's starting calories.
- Cooking oil and butter: include the amount normally used. A consistent measured amount is more important than pretending every retained gram can be known exactly.
- Marinades and sauces: include the retained or plated amount, not automatically the entire preparation batch.

## Sauce System

PRPD may rotate two sauces weekly without editing every meal recipe.

1. Create one controlled record per sauce.
2. Standardize the plated grams per sauce cup.
3. Calculate calories and macros for that plated amount once.
4. Test every permitted two-sauce pairing.
5. If all pairings remain inside a narrow agreed range, publish one conservative two-sauce allowance. If a pairing exceeds the allowance, use that pairing's actual value.

Do not assume yogurt, chipotle, cilantro-lime, teriyaki, and mayo-containing sauces are equivalent until their plated amounts are measured.

## Wrapped-Meal Capacity Rule

Wrapped dishes do not automatically inherit the same Bulk protein quantity as bowls.

- Build one Lean and one Bulk test wrap.
- Record cooked protein grams, total filling grams, closure quality, packaged weight, and reheated texture.
- If the Bulk target does not close reliably, choose a larger Bulk wrap, two smaller wraps, or a documented lower wrapped-meal Bulk target.
- Do not compress an oversized portion into one wrap merely to preserve a historical protein target.

## Source Priority

1. Current product package photographed by PRPD
2. Current manufacturer nutrition page
3. USDA FoodData Central entry appropriate to the exact food state
4. Existing PRPD estimate, marked Draft until replaced

## Taste-First Decision Rule

If a meal misses a historical macro target but tastes substantially better, keep the better-tasting version and publish its new working estimate. Protein should remain meaningful for the meal category, but it is not the only measure of quality.

Use rounded customer-facing values. Calories may be rounded to a practical interval such as the nearest 5 or 10 calories; protein may be rounded to the nearest gram. Do not describe the values as lab verified.

## Work Queue

1. Current active menu dishes
2. Reusable sauces and shared components
3. Frequently repeated recipes
4. Historical library recipes before they return to a weekly menu
