# PRPD Cook-Day Checklist

Use this with the approved recipes and the final order count. The purpose is to remove guessing, prevent shortages, and keep macro portions consistent.

## After Order Cutoff

- [ ] Export the final order list.
- [ ] Count every dish by Lean, Bulk, and dessert quantity.
- [ ] Reconcile the count to Orders and Payment Log.
- [ ] Flag dietary restrictions and special notes separately.
- [ ] Convert dish counts into component portions: proteins, starches, vegetables, sauces, garnishes, and desserts.
- [ ] Convert component portions into required ready-to-cook quantities using the approved recipe yield.
- [ ] Record any controlled production buffer explicitly. Do not create an unmeasured "extra" batch.
- [ ] Create the shopping list and check inventory before purchasing.

## Day Before Production

- [ ] Confirm every ingredient and packaging item is on hand.
- [ ] Confirm labels, lids, sauce cups, bags, and delivery supplies.
- [ ] Print the production count sheet and recipe cards.
- [ ] Print or stage labels only from approved dish data.
- [ ] Group ingredients by station/component, not by customer.
- [ ] Pre-weigh dry spice blends and label each batch.
- [ ] Charge thermometers and verify scales with a known weight.
- [ ] Confirm refrigerator space for shallow-pan cooling and finished meals.

## Opening Checks

- [ ] Record date, batch number, delivery date, and team members.
- [ ] Record refrigerator and freezer temperatures.
- [ ] Wash hands; clean and sanitize work areas and food-contact equipment.
- [ ] Set out sanitized probes, scales, timers, sheet pans, and labeled holding containers.
- [ ] Review the production sequence and equipment bottlenecks.
- [ ] Mark yesterday's completed desserts, sauces, and rice in the planner; verify count and cold holding instead of remaking them.
- [ ] Assign three visible lanes: passive equipment, active cooking, and cold/cooling.

## Phase Production

- [ ] Stage labeled destination pans for every selected protein before the first raw batch is opened.
- [ ] Cook every protein in one continuous block: breakfast protein, chicken, ground beef/formed beef, steak, then seafood, following the planner's exact order.
- [ ] Do not assemble, plate, cook eggs, mix dough, or start ready-to-eat component work during the protein block.
- [ ] Limit hands-on simultaneous tasks to the number the team can actively monitor; with two people, default to one active protein task alongside passive protein equipment.
- [ ] Keep every marinade and finished dish in its own labeled bowl or pan after any genuinely shared base preparation.
- [ ] Split batches to the actual pan, oven, skillet, or griddle capacity. Do not overcrowd equipment to save a cycle.
- [ ] For every protein, record starting weight, endpoint temperature, count, actual cooked yield, reserve used, destination pan, and owner before advancing.
- [ ] Reconcile every raw pull and cooked destination pan before closing the protein block.
- [ ] Remove raw tools and containers; wash, rinse, and sanitize the station; change gloves/apron as required; mark the raw-protein station **CLOSED**.
- [ ] After closure, work by component family: breakfast components, starches, vegetables, sauces, doughs/fillings, then assembly.
- [ ] Do not begin final assembly until every required component for the selected dish has been released.

## Component Production

For every component:

- [ ] Record recipe/version and planned portions.
- [ ] Record any ingredient brand, supplier, or package substitution that changes nutrition, allergens, yield, or cost.
- [ ] Record ready-to-cook starting weight.
- [ ] Follow the approved method, equipment load, time, and temperature.
- [ ] Record final cooking temperature where required.
- [ ] Record cooked yield before portioning.
- [ ] Record measured liquid oil and salt added to the component when the controlled recipe does not already specify them.
- [ ] Compare actual yield with planned yield before assembly begins.
- [ ] If yield is short, stop and calculate the correction. Do not silently reduce portions.

## Cooling Controls

- [ ] Start the cooling clock when cooked TCS food reaches 135 F.
- [ ] Cool from 135 F to 70 F within two hours.
- [ ] Continue cooling to 41 F or below within six total hours.
- [ ] Use shallow pans, smaller portions, ice baths, rapid-cooling equipment, or another approved method.
- [ ] Record time, temperature, initials, method, and any corrective action.

Texas cooling log: https://www.dshs.texas.gov/sites/default/files/foodestablishments/pdf/GuidanceDocs/F23-13356CoolingofCookedPHF.pdf

## Assembly Line

- [ ] Confirm each component's total cooked yield before opening containers.
- [ ] Set a labeled scale station for each component.
- [ ] Keep Lean and Bulk specifications visible.
- [ ] Tare every container before portioning.
- [ ] Weigh the first three portions of each tier.
- [ ] Check a portion at regular intervals and the final three portions.
- [ ] Keep sauces and garnishes in controlled weights or measured tools.
- [ ] Record rejected/reworked containers instead of hiding them in the count.
- [ ] Complete one full dish/tier run before changing the line. Reconcile Lean, reset, then run Bulk.
- [ ] Bag by customer only after all dish/tier runs are complete and reconciled.

## Packaging and Labeling

- [ ] Confirm dish name and Lean/Bulk tier.
- [ ] Confirm correct macro label only after the recipe is approved.
- [ ] Apply made/use-by or date-marking information.
- [ ] Apply allergen information required by the approved label system.
- [ ] Confirm storage and reheating instructions.
- [ ] Check label adhesion after the container is cold and dry.

## Final Reconciliation

- [ ] Count finished meals by dish and tier.
- [ ] Record planned, prepared, packed, leftover, and wasted quantities separately. These numbers must reconcile.
- [ ] Match packed counts to the final customer order list.
- [ ] Match desserts and sauce cups separately.
- [ ] Record extra, short, damaged, or reworked units.
- [ ] Stage each customer order with a final name/count check.
- [ ] Record final cold-holding temperature before delivery loading.
- [ ] Clean, sanitize, and close the production area.

## Post-Cook Review

- [ ] Record actual ingredient use and leftovers.
- [ ] Record actual yield percentage for each major component.
- [ ] Record which recipe, equipment, or timing assumption failed.
- [ ] Update the recipe only after reviewing the recorded evidence.
- [ ] Keep this week's records with the batch number and delivery date.
- [ ] Complete `BATCH_PRODUCTION_RECORD_TEMPLATE.md`; do not rely on chat or memory as the permanent batch record.
- [ ] For every new or changed dish, record fresh taste, refrigerated quality, customer-style reheat quality, and the resulting Retest / Provisional / Approved decision.
