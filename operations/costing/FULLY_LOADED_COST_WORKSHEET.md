## Calculations

### Direct packed cost

`customer meal ingredients and packaging + shared QC meals + waste/replacements`

### Labor cost

`(prep + cook/pack + cleaning + administration + delivery hours) x management labor rate`

Use a real management labor rate even when an owner is not currently taking payroll. Otherwise the business can appear profitable only because owner labor is treated as free.

### Delivery cash cost

`route miles / 24.4 MPG x fuel price per gallon`

This measures cash fuel only. Keep a separate vehicle-cost benchmark for maintenance, tires, depreciation, insurance, and repairs.

### Fully loaded weekly cost

`direct packed cost + labor + kitchen + unincluded utilities + delivery + payment fees + weekly overhead`

### Estimated weekly operating profit

`meal revenue + delivery fees collected - fully loaded weekly cost`

### Fully loaded cost per customer meal

`fully loaded weekly cost / customer meals sold`

### Fully loaded margin

`estimated weekly operating profit / total weekly revenue x 100`

## Allocation Examples

- A $50 delivery route spread across 50 meals adds $1.00 per meal.
- The same $50 route spread across 100 meals adds $0.50 per meal.
- Twenty hours valued at $20/hour across 100 meals adds $4.00 labor per meal.
- A $100 kitchen charge across 100 meals adds $1.00 per meal.

This is why a 30% direct packed cost can still produce a small final profit margin. Weekly volume and labor efficiency matter as much as ingredient price.

## Current Missing Inputs

PRPD cannot calculate a defensible fully loaded margin until these are recorded:

1. Total weekly hours by work category and number of people working.
2. Chosen management labor rate.
3. Commercial-kitchen charge and utilities included.
4. Actual customer meal count and weekly revenue.
5. Actual delivery miles, fuel price, and delivery fees collected.
6. Consumables not yet assigned per meal.
7. Weekly or monthly overhead expenses.
