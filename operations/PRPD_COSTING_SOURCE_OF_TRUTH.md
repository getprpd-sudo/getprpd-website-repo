## What the Percentages Mean

`Direct packed cost % = ingredients and known meal-specific packaging / selling price`

Example: a meal with a $3.00 direct packed cost sold for $10.00 has a 30% direct packed cost. The current model already includes the pooled raw-protein reserve in that $3.00. The remaining $7.00 is not profit because it must still pay for labor, commercial-kitchen expense, delivery, utilities, payment fees, spoilage, and operating overhead.

The current-menu unweighted average of 29.0% means the modeled recipe builds consume approximately 29 cents of every sales dollar in ingredients, the pooled raw-protein reserve, and direct meal-specific packaging/consumables. It does not mean PRPD earns 71% profit. This average is still preliminary because some ingredients use planning prices and actual kitchen yields must be recorded. The current production policy adds a 5% pooled reserve to raw meat and poultry rather than producing automatic finished QC meals.

The earlier 39.5% next-menu planning example is superseded. The rebuilt July 25 controlled-recipe report currently averages 30.1% direct packed cost including the 5% pooled raw-protein reserve and before order-level delivery allocation. Treat that as a planning-grade menu-comparison figure until remaining package prices and kitchen yields are confirmed.

## Required Cost Layers

### 1. Recipe food cost

- Exact raw ingredient grams multiplied by current unit cost
- Marinades, sauces, seasonings, spray oil, garnishes, sides, and condiments
- Raw-to-cooked yield and unavoidable trim/drain loss
- One shared PRPD quality/yield serving per selected dish, allocated across customer servings

### 2. Packed customer-meal cost

- Recipe food cost
- Container and lid
- Sauce/side cups and lids
- Foil, parchment, bag, wrap, and other meal-specific consumables
- Label at current cash cost and separate replacement-cost view

### 3. Fully loaded cost

- Packed customer-meal cost
- Prep, cook, packing, administration, cleaning, and delivery labor
- Commercial-kitchen rental
- Water/electricity/gas only when not already included in kitchen rent
- Vehicle expense
- Payment-processing fees
- Spoilage, refunds, test batches, and unsold product
- Allocated insurance, permits, software, domain, phone, marketing, and other monthly overhead

Do not double-count utilities included in kitchen rent or vehicle expenses already represented by a mileage allowance.

For the current startup cash view, unpaid owner labor is reported separately and is not deducted from cash retained in the business bank account. This does not make the time economically free; it simply keeps the immediate cash view separate from a future sustainable-owner-pay view. Commercial-kitchen expense is $0 unless PRPD actually pays rent or usage fees. Utilities are $0 as a separate line when they are included in kitchen access or not billed to PRPD.

## Delivery Cost

`route miles / 24 MPG x actual price per gallon`

Allocate delivery at the order level first, then report average delivery cost per delivered meal. Recipe food cost should remain independent of route length.
